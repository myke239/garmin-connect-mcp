# Workout preparation decisions

Integrate the existing recovery/strength tools with practical constraints. Do not
create a new weighted health score. Distinguish reported observations, calculations
and coaching judgment. Favor personal baselines and comparable sessions over
universal wearable cutoffs. Inadequate baseline means uncertainty, not normality.

| Domain | Consider | Abstention/fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | Remaining session order, purpose, duration, intensity, start time, distance/terrain, exact strength exercises | Ask for start/duration; provide conditional advice meanwhile |
| Sleep | Last night, several-night pattern, wake time, naps, usual sleep opportunity | Missing watch night is not zero sleep |
| Physiology | HRV/resting HR against same-source history, relevant respiration trends | Do not diagnose illness or infer local muscle recovery |
| Garmin composites | Morning readiness, stress, Body Battery with measurement context | Collector currently provides morning readiness, not current noon readiness; Body Battery scalar normalization is unverified |
| Subjective | Fatigue, regional soreness, pain, illness, psychological stress | Unknown stays unknown; concerning symptoms outweigh favorable scores |
| Strength demand | Exercise/equipment, work sets, effort, sequence overlap, regional symptoms | Raw weights, missing set times/RIR cannot become verified exposure inputs |
| Nutrition | Yesterday, today's consumed versus prelogged food, meal time, carbs/protein, goal and workout demand | Incomplete diary cannot establish intake, glycogen or energy availability |
| Hydration/environment | Duration, heat/humidity, known sweat experience, access to fluids | Logged fluid alone does not establish hydration; no invented sodium/sweat targets |
| Practicality | Food preferences/allergies, GI tolerance, available time, work commitments | Use familiar feasible options; don't propose an impossible meal/nap window |

## Food timing

Use current primary sports-nutrition guidance and the user's established plan.
For sustained endurance exercise, guidance commonly describes carbohydrate intake
of 1-4 g/kg in the 1-4 hours before exercise; this is a contextual range, not a
universal dose. Select a justified narrower option only with body mass, actual
time available, expected duration/intensity, recent consumed food and tolerance.
Show the calculation and relevant timing. Do not recommend the full range on top
of food already consumed in that window. Distinguish planned diary entries from
eaten food. If timing or body mass is missing, provide food-based conditional
options and ask for the missing information instead of inventing grams.

Longer sessions may need a during-session fueling plan; account for the user's
practiced tolerance and existing plan. Do not automatically transfer an endurance
regimen to a short strength workout. Review protein distribution and post-session
food when useful, without treating every session as requiring supplements. Do not
derive daily caloric needs or calorie deficits from wearable expenditure alone.

Reference: [ISSN nutrient timing position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28919842/).
The agent should verify precise recommendations against applicable primary guidance
before presenting quantities; medical conditions or a clinician's plan can change
what is appropriate.

## Rest and workout options

A short night prompts review of recent sleep, symptoms and the planned demand.
A short nap may help if there is enough time to wake fully and it will not disrupt
nighttime sleep. Do not automatically prescribe 30 minutes, promise that a nap
restores readiness, or recommend stimulant/supplement changes. Consider earlier
bedtime, a practical break and moving or reducing the workout where appropriate.

Reference: [Athlete sleep consensus](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/55/7/356).

Offer a small number of conditional options with reasons: proceed as planned,
adjust duration/intensity, reassess during warmup, or postpone. Preserve the user's
training goal and distinguish aerobic from local strength recovery. Pain/illness
may require professional assessment; wearable scores are not medical clearance.
If the scheduled run is late, do not send pre-exercise instructions for a workout
already completed. Report the missed preparation window and focus on remaining
sessions or recovery.
